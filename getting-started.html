Getting Started

Part 1 explains how mSpendVanta thinks about your money. Parts 2 to 4 take you from your first import to tracking bills and building a budget.

Want to look around first? mSpendVanta opens on sample data — a full set of accounts, transactions, bills and budgets — so you can try every screen before importing anything of your own.

Part 1

How mSpendVanta works

A few ideas shape the whole app. Knowing them makes everything else quicker.

Why mSpendVanta

Most of us see our money one statement, one receipt and one surprise bill at a time. Up close, it's hard to tell whether things are on track. mSpendVanta steps you back to the view that matters: what you spend, how it compares with your budget, and which bills are coming.

It's deliberately focused on spending. Many finance apps go much further, into net worth, investments and income, and ask you to connect every account to do it. mSpendVanta leaves those out. What you get is a short routine you can keep up: import, categorize, review.

Privacy by design

Your financial history says a great deal about you, so mSpendVanta is built to keep it yours:

  • No bank logins. The app never asks for your bank password and never connects to your bank. It works from the statement files your bank already lets you download.
  • No servers of ours. Your data lives on your Mac and in a private iCloud database that belongs to your Apple Account, which is how it reaches your iPhone. We can't see it.
  • No accounts, ads or tracking. There's nothing to sign up for, and nothing about you is collected.

The one exception you can choose is AI suggestions, which can use Apple's private cloud. See AI suggestions for exactly what that sends.

Bank files: use OFX or QFX

Your bank's website lets you download your transactions, usually under Download, Export or Statements. Choose OFX or QFX (sometimes labelled "Quicken" or "Microsoft Money") whenever you can. These files:

  • identify the account, so mSpendVanta sets the bank up for you;
  • include your current balance;
  • give each transaction an ID, so duplicates are caught reliably;
  • keep the payee and the memo apart, which makes categorizing cleaner.

Use CSV only when your bank offers nothing else. It works, but you'll match the columns the first time, and it carries no balance.

Pick one format per account and stick with it. Switching between CSV and OFX for the same account is the most common cause of duplicate transactions.

Categorizing: vendors, categories and match text

Every transaction gets a category — what the money was for, like Groceries or Utilities. It can also get a vendor — who you paid, like Whole Foods. Vendors are optional: a cash withdrawal or a one-off purchase may need only a category. A vendor has a default category, so choosing Whole Foods fills in Groceries for you.

To recognize a transaction, mSpendVanta uses the description and memo from your bank, which often look like this:

SQ *BLUE BOTTLE COFFEE #12 DENVER CO

When you categorize, you choose the match text: the part of that text that identifies the merchant. Getting it right is what makes future imports sort themselves.

Match textResult
BLUE BOTTLE COFFEEGood. Specific to the merchant, and matches every visit.
COFFEEToo broad. Catches every coffee shop.
BLUE BOTTLE COFFEE #12 DENVER COToo specific. Misses a visit to a different store or city.

Leave out things that change from charge to charge — dates, reference numbers, card numbers. mSpendVanta suggests match text for you and shows how many transactions it matches, so you can check before saving.

Mapping rules

When you categorize a transaction for All Transactions, mSpendVanta saves a mapping rule: the match text, plus the vendor and category it stands for. From then on, every import categorizes matching transactions automatically. Only merchants you haven't seen before wait for you.

That's why the first import takes the longest and each one after is quicker. You can view, edit or delete rules from a vendor or category (Account Setup › Vendors › Mapping Rules), but you rarely need to.

AI suggestions

A first import can bring in thousands of transactions. On Macs with Apple Intelligence, mSpendVanta can suggest the vendor, category and match text for each new merchant, so you review and confirm instead of typing. It's the quickest way through a large import.

Apple's Private Cloud Compute (recommended)

Apple's larger cloud model gives noticeably better suggestions, especially for recognizing vendors. To answer, it receives only the transaction's description and memo, a few similar past transactions with the vendor and category you gave them, and your vendor and category names so it can choose from them — never amounts, dates, balances or account details. Apple uses this to answer, doesn't store it, and doesn't make it available to anyone — including Apple. It's on by default; Apple sets a daily limit, after which the on-device model takes over.

On your Mac only

Apple's on-device model runs entirely on your Mac and nothing leaves it. It's a smaller model: good at categories, less reliable at picking the right vendor.

No AI at all

If you'd rather nothing be suggested, turn suggestions off and categorize by hand. Your mapping rules still learn as you go.

Choose in Settings › AI Suggestions.

Part 2

Import and categorize your transactions

Import your bank files

  1. On your bank's website, download your transactions as OFX or QFX (or CSV if that's all there is). The last 30 days is plenty for a routine import; for your first, download as much history as you'd like to see.
  2. In mSpendVanta, click Import at the bottom of the sidebar and choose the file.
  3. OFX or QFX: mSpendVanta recognizes the account, or sets up a new bank for it, and shows the transactions. Click Import.
    CSV: choose the bank, then match the columns — date, description, a second description or memo if there is one, and amount. mSpendVanta remembers them for next time.
  4. The summary shows how many transactions were imported, how many were already there and skipped, and how many your rules categorized.

Make it a weekly routine. Every week or so, download the latest 30 days and import. Overlapping downloads are fine — transactions already imported are recognized and skipped, so there's nothing to keep track of. Your balances update each time.

Imported the wrong file? Delete it in Transactions › Import History.

Work through Unmatched

Anything your rules don't recognize waits in Categorize › Unmatched. You can view it two ways:

  • By Merchant groups repeat charges from the same merchant. Start here: one decision categorizes every transaction in the group, and its rule catches the next ones.
  • By Transaction lists each one on its own — for one-off purchases and anything left over.

Three ways to categorize

  • AI Wizard takes you through them one at a time with suggestions ready. Adjust if needed, then Assign — or Skip to come back later. The fastest way through a big import.
  • Ask AI on a row gets a suggestion for just that merchant or transaction.
  • Categorize… opens the full editor to choose everything yourself.

Reviewing an AI suggestion

The suggestion shows a vendor and category, the match text and the bank. Change the vendor or category from its menu, and trim the match text if needed — the line below it turns green when it matches every transaction in the group. Then:

  • Accept on the vendor-and-category row saves both, with a rule;
  • Accept on the Category-only row saves just the category, without a vendor.

Categorizing by hand

  1. Click Categorize… on the row.
  2. Choose a Vendor (optional) and a Category (required). Picking a vendor fills in its default category.
  3. Trim the Mapping Text to the part that identifies the merchant, and check it still shows Matches Transaction.
  4. Choose All Transactions to save it as a rule, or This Transaction for a one-off. Then Save.

For a quick pick, use the category and vendor icons beside Categorize…, or right-click a row for Quick Category and Quick Vendor.

Create and edit vendors and categories

You'll create most of them while categorizing. In any vendor or category menu, choose Add/Edit… and start typing: your existing names appear as you type, and Tab fills one in. Use an existing name where you can, so you don't end up with "Starbucks" and "Starbucks Coffee". A new vendor needs a category, which becomes its default.

To tidy up later, go to Account Setup › Vendors or Categories. There you can rename, change a vendor's default category, see its transactions and rules, Merge… duplicates, or delete one — its transactions keep their category.

Ignore what isn't spending

Transfers between your own accounts, credit card payments and deposits aren't spending. Ignore them so the same money isn't counted twice. Right-click a transaction in Unmatched and choose:

  • Ignore Transaction Only — just this one.
  • Ignore Description Going Forward — a rule that ignores this one, and future ones like it.

The Categorize editor and the AI Wizard have Ignore buttons too.

All banks, or one bank?

A rule can apply to all your banks or only the one the transaction came from. Use All Banks when the text names a merchant. Use this bank for generic bank wording like ONLINE TRANSFER or PAYMENT THANK YOU, which can mean different things at different banks. mSpendVanta picks the sensible one for you.

To review or undo an ignore rule, go to Transactions › Ignore Rules.

Part 3

Track your bills

Because mSpendVanta has your transaction history, it can find your recurring payments — rent, utilities, subscriptions, loans, insurance — and turn them into bills you can follow through the month.

  • Auto Pay bills are paid automatically. mSpendVanta matches each payment as you import it, and flags any that come in higher than expected.
  • Manual bills are ones you pay yourself. mSpendVanta shows what's due so you remember to schedule the payment, lets you mark it paid, and lets you update the amount when the bill changes.

Create your bills

  1. Go to Budgeting › Bills and click Create Bills….
  2. mSpendVanta lists the recurring payments it found that don't have a bill yet. Switch off any you don't want, then create them.
  3. Open a bill to set its amount, due day, the months it's due, and whether it's Auto Pay or manual.
  4. For a bill it didn't find — a new one, or one paid only certain months of the year — click New Bill….

Then check Reporting › Bill Payments to see what's paid, what's coming, and what still needs scheduling.

Part 4

Build a budget

mSpendVanta budgets your spending in two parts:

  • Recurring payments — bills, subscriptions and loans, paid on a schedule at much the same amount. Each is budgeted for the months it's due.
  • Everyday spending — groceries, dining, fuel and the like, which varies. Each category gets a monthly amount.

Rather than starting from a blank page, mSpendVanta builds a budget from what you actually spent, which you then adjust.

Create a budget template

  1. Go to Budgeting › Templates and click New Template…. Choose the history to start from, and optionally adjust for price increases and round the amounts.
  2. Review each category. Set its everyday amount — use the history average, or 10% less or more — and check its recurring payments. Switch off anything that isn't really recurring. Recurring Payments at the top shows them all in one list.
  3. Assign the template to a year. One template can be reused for several years.

Then open Reporting › Budget Performance to compare what you spent with the budget, month by month — where you're over, why, and where the year is heading.
